Amends [ADR-001](adr-001.md) Annex A.2 (producer rules) and A.3 (role table). Companion: ADR-004 (audit log). *Paraphrases: "A6.1", "A6.2", "A6.3", "Invariant check", "Acceptance tests".*

## Problem

The audit log design needs three things ADR-001's role and outbox rules did not allow: a role to erase audit diffs, tenant onboarding that can seed the audit sealer, and an outbox start row for a workflow (the sealer) that is not about a record.

## Decision

- **A6.1:** a new database role `nava_erasure`, a one-shot job role. It holds no membership, ownership or `BYPASSRLS`; it can read audit columns only (never the diff or its salt), perform the erase transition, and record its own erase entry. Its credential lives with the job runner, never on an app or worker host.
- **A6.2:** `nava_onboard` gets `INSERT` on `outbox` (only rows of kind `audit.seal`, for a tenant that is still provisioning, enforced by the policy) and on `audit_chain_head`. No `SELECT` on `outbox`. Seeding is idempotent through the head's primary key.
- **A6.3:** a start row not tied to a record takes `target_seq` from its own head row's version under that row's lock, and inserts no `workflow_ref`. Everything else in A.2 still applies.

Not part of this amendment: allowing `audit.seal` in the Temporal workload allow-list, which is a separate policy change under [A1](adr-001-a1.md).

## Consequences

D22, the workflow-id scheme, the relay and the 10-entry policy allow-list are unchanged. I1, I2, I3, I6 and I7 hold.

## Accepted risks

Whoever holds the erasure credential can erase and record a valid erase entry; that shows up as an erasure but is still loss of evidence.
