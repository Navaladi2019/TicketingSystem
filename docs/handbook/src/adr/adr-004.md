## Problem

*Paraphrases: "Decision", "Consequences".* The product needs one history of record changes and configuration changes that a buyer can export and check for tampering, under any filter, without trusting the operator's word.

## Decision

*Paraphrases: "Decision" 1 to 6.*

- **One log.** A single append-only table, `audit_log`, with three domains: `record`, `config` and `audit` (exports, erasures, reseeds). One writer (`audit.Append`, inside the caller's transaction), one reader and one export.
- **The database checks the same-transaction rule.** Every audited table gets a deferred constraint trigger. At COMMIT it requires an entry for the same tenant, transaction and subject. A raw write that skips `audit.Append` fails.
- **Tamper evidence.** A per-tenant hash chain, `entry_hash = SHA-256(prev_hash ‖ envelope_sha256)`, ordered by `seq`. A self-rescheduling sealer assigns `seq` asynchronously; writers never wait on it.
- **Exports verify offline under any filter.** Entries a filter excludes appear as hash-only stubs, and the range is anchored one entry before the window. The verifier ships as `nava audit verify <file>`.
- **Erasure** is one of exactly two transitions allowed on a written row, and only the `nava_erasure` role may make it. The chain covers a salted diff hash, so it still verifies after erasure.
- **S8's published versions are backfilled** into the log by two migrations; they are not exposed through a view.

## Rules that matter most

*Paraphrases: "Rules", R1 to R28.*

| Area | What the ADR fixes |
|---|---|
| Content (R1 to R4) | The envelope columns, closed vocabularies for `action`, `source` and `subject_type`, versioned configuration with content hashes, and how AI and change-set actions are attributed. |
| Secrets and masks (R5 to R8) | One diff walker. A secret at an `x-nava-secret` or `writeOnly` path is rejected at write time. Masks are applied at read time. Reading needs the standalone `audit.read` or `audit.export` permission. |
| Isolation and immutability (R9 to R12) | `FORCE` row-level security, no DELETE grant for any role, INSERT asserted (never rewritten) by trigger, exactly two UPDATE transitions (seal, erase), writer grants generated from `db/audited-tables.yaml`. |
| Chain and sealer (R13 to R18) | The hash definition and golden vectors, one `audit_chain_head` row per tenant, `seq` rules, the self-rescheduling sealer, and `chain:reseed` (409 `chain_alive` unless the chain looks dead for 10 minutes). |
| Export (R19 to R22) | One export per tenant at a time, the anchored range, paging, JSONL and CSV formats with formula-prefix protection. |
| Erasure (R23, R24) | The erase step for Epic 30 and the verifier outcomes for erased entries (`tampered` or `pending` unless a chained erase entry names them). |
| Backfill and storage (R25 to R28) | The order of migrations A and B, the access path for the backfill, lock order, and the partitioned key shape. |

## Options that lost

*Paraphrases: "Rejected options".* Separate record and configuration logs; a view over S8 versions instead of a backfill; chaining inside the writer's transaction; a shared seal key per minute (writers queued 3.9 to 4.0 s in two spikes); one seal start per transaction; an eternal per-tenant workflow; triggers that write the audit rows (they cannot know the actor or AI decision); `jsonb` diffs (the stored bytes would no longer match their hash); a single `REPEATABLE READ` export snapshot; external anchoring in the first milestone; a dedicated sealer role.

## Consequences

*Paraphrases: "Consequences".* Each configuration epic registers its subject types and manifest rows. Entries carry `sealed: false` until the sealer ships. Sealing roughly doubles heap WAL on `audit_log`. The sealer needs an outbox producer entry point not tied to a record.

## Accepted risks

*Paraphrases: "Accepted risks".*

| # | Risk | Revisit trigger |
|---|---|---|
| R-A1 | A consistent whole-chain rewrite by the owner is detected only against an export the tenant already holds. External anchoring was deferred. | A regulated buyer asks for external attestation. |
| R-A2 | Sealing lag: about 10 s when the tenant is active, up to 300 s after idle. | The `audit_unsealed_oldest_seconds` alert fires in production. |
| R-A4 | Commit-trigger, sealing-WAL and deferred-queue costs were unmeasured when accepted (spike S1). | The S1 result. |
| R-A5 | Idle-tenant seal starts add Temporal load. | Starts above 10% of Temporal capacity. |
| R-A6 | Migration B holds ACCESS EXCLUSIVE locks across its transaction. | S8 volume at planning. |
| (R8) | A compromised worker credential can seal or insert for any tenant. Already accepted in ADR-001. | The ADR-001 R8 trigger. |

## Acceptance tests

*Paraphrases: "Acceptance tests", CI-A1 to CI-A16.* They prove, among other things: no entry survives a rollback; a raw write to an audited table fails at COMMIT; read, export and reseed never cross tenants; export verification passes under every filter and fails on an edited byte, a deleted row or a deleted first entry; erasure verifies; the backfill leaves one entry per S8 version; a killed sealer is recovered by the next run.
