Story S5: group changes and deprovisioning take effect within 10 minutes

A plain-language explainer of what this story built, why it matters, how we know it works, and what to check before you accept it. You don't need to know anything about the project to follow it.

In one paragraph. People log in through a separate login system (Keycloak). That system decides who belongs to which customer organisation and which teams they're in. This story copies that information into our own database every 5 minutes. The API checks the copy on every request, not the person's login ticket. So when an administrator removes someone from a team, or switches off their account, the change takes effect within 10 minutes, even for a login ticket that hasn't expired yet. It also adds a set of automated "attacks": tests that try to trick the platform into letting someone from one customer into another customer's data. Every attack is refused.

1. The problem, in everyday terms

Think of an office building with many companies inside (each company is a tenant). There's one security desk at the entrance, Keycloak, which hands out visitor badges (login tokens). A badge says "Alice, Company A" and is valid for a few minutes.

Inside the building, each company's rooms are locked by our platform. The question this story answers is: what happens when Company A fires Alice, or moves her out of the Support team?

The risk

If our rooms trusted the badge alone, Alice could keep walking in until her badge expired, and the badge could even claim teams she has since left.

What this story does

Our platform keeps its own up-to-date staff list per company, refreshed every 5 minutes, and checks it at every door. A removed or disabled person loses access within 10 minutes, whatever their badge says.

Because one building hosts many companies, a second question matters just as much: can anyone get a badge for a company they don't belong to? Section 6 lists every trick we tried. All of them fail.

3. The big picture

Two loops run side by side. The sync loop (top) keeps our records fresh. The request loop (bottom) checks those records on every call.

The key design choice: the API never takes your teams or your "switched on" status from the badge. Your company comes from the badge (through the company binding). Everything else comes from our records.

    5. Why "within 10 minutes" holds

    Two clocks matter. Both are capped at 5 minutes, and the tests check both caps.

    • Sync interval ≤ 5 minutes. The worker refuses a configured interval above 5 minutes (config.MaxGroupsyncInterval). Its default is 5 minutes.
    • Badge lifetime ≤ 5 minutes. Keycloak's accessTokenLifespan is 300 seconds.
    • In practice the change lands after one sync, so within 5 minutes. The API reads teams and the enabled flag from our records on every request, so an unexpired badge already sees the change. The 10-minute figure is the documented, safe upper bound.
    • If sync falls behind, the worker reports groupsync_lag_seconds and names any company that's more than 10 minutes behind in a log line, so operations can see it.

    How do we know the tamper test (CI-5.06) would catch the real bug?

    Older Keycloak versions (26.0.0 to 26.1.2) really did have this bug. We keep a pinned copy of version 26.1.2 and run the same test against it, where the tamper must succeed (make identity-attacks-fixture). Last run on 2026-10-26: the tamper worked on 26.1.2 and failed on our version, 26.8.1. So the test can see the bug, and our version doesn't have it.

    What about administrators and the platform operator?

    The operator (who runs the whole building) and company administrators get different badges for different doors. A company admin's badge gets 403 on operator routes (/api/v1/operator/*). An operator's badge gets 401 on a company route. Other tests check that people can't edit their own company, role or team through the self-service profile page, can't give themselves the operator role, can't swap their badge for another (token exchange is off), and that machine accounts can't act inside any company.

    9. Known limits, honestly

    • Partly proven The 403 for company admins on operator routes. The operator routes were built in story S4, on a separate branch. On this branch the route doesn't exist yet, so the test proves the admin's badge is refused and the route isn't served (404). When S4 and S5 meet on main, the test must be switched to require 403 (tracked as TODO-010). The test is built to fail as soon as the route appears, so this can't be forgotten.
    • Accepted risk AR-039 (low) Self-service email change. If the account console were ever re-enabled, a user could change their own email without verification. That can't change their company, role or teams (tested), but it could block another company from inviting that address. Recommended fix at the login-system layer is recorded.
    • Deferred, with a trigger TICK-220. When a person leaves Keycloak, the worker deletes their row. That's fine today. The first future table that points at a person without a delete rule would break sync for that company. The rule must be decided before any such table merges.
    • Fixed The attack suite now runs in GitHub Actions on every PR and keeps the ci5-report artifact (TODO-011).

    10. Try it yourself (about 15 minutes)

    The full step-by-step kit, with the exact commands and the evidence of every check, is docs/acceptance/s5-group-changes-within-10-minutes.md. QA verified 2026-10-27 at 0135a08: all criteria pass In short:

    1. Start the stack, seed Company A and Company B (one SQL block in the kit), and run nava-groupsync --once. Sign in as the seeded user alice@tenant-a.test (Company A) and call GET /api/v1/me. group_paths shows her team, /service-desk. (Her eng/platform is a department, an org unit, so it isn't listed.)
    2. In the Keycloak admin console, remove Alice from service-desk (Groups, then t, Company A's id, service-desk, Members).
    3. Call GET /api/v1/me again with the same token. After the next sync, group_paths is empty. QA measured 244 s with the worker on its 5-minute default; the promise is 10 minutes. Run nava-groupsync --once to skip the wait.
    4. Disable Alice in the admin console. She can't sign in again at once, and after the next sync her existing token gets 401 (QA: 266 s, with the token not yet expired).
    5. Re-enable Alice (the attack tests sign in as her), then run make identity-attacks and open ci5-report.md: 14 lines, all PASS.

    11. Glossary

    Tenant
    One customer organisation using the platform. Its data is walled off from every other tenant's.
    Keycloak / realm / organisation
    The login system. A realm is one login space (ours is nava). Inside it, each tenant is a Keycloak "organisation".
    Token (access token / ID token)
    The short-lived signed badge you present to the API. An ID token only says who you are and must never be accepted as an access token.
    IdP (identity provider) / brokered login
    A company's own login system (for example their corporate sign-in) that Keycloak trusts for that company's users. "Foreign IdP" means another company's.
    Group / org unit
    Teams and departments. In Keycloak they sit under /t/<tenant id>/…. The sync worker refuses anything outside the tenant's own path.
    Deprovisioning
    Removing someone's access: taking them off a team, or disabling their account.
    401 / 403
    401 means "we don't accept who you are here". 403 means "we know who you are, but you may not do this".
    CI
    The automated test pipeline that runs on every proposed code change. A failing check blocks the merge.
