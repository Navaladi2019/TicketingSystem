ADR-008 explained: a Competitive Gate in every design decision

DecisionEvery new design record (ADR) gets a short Competitive Gate section. It says which of four measures the design improves against Salesforce, ServiceNow and Freshworks, by how much and how that is measured, and which of their known problems it avoids. A design with no number, or one that repeats a known problem without an accepted reason, is sent back.
IssueTICK-857. The founder asked that every design show how it makes us "10x better" than those three products.
StatusPROPOSED, 2026-11-15. Technical Architect: critique round 1 found two blocking problems. Both were fixed in RFC rev 1, and the re-check approved it.
Not decided herePricing targets, and whether we may benchmark competitor trial accounts. Both go to the human.

In plain words

1. Goal

Make every design decision push us toward being much better than Salesforce Service Cloud, ServiceNow and Freshworks, and make that push checkable rather than a slogan.

2. Today

Design records check isolation, reliability and correctness, but they say nothing about competitors. "Ten times better" cannot be reviewed as written: nobody can tell whether a design meets it.

3. What's new

  • A register of 17 sourced competitor problems. Each has an ID, a link and an evidence grade. Four of the starting guesses did not hold up and were dropped. For example, Freshworks is quick to start, so we do not claim to beat them there.
  • Four measures where our architecture can really be far ahead (below). Each has a number, a baseline and a way to measure it.
  • A four-line section in every ADR: the measure moved, by how much, the problems avoided or risked, and how it is measured. We may say "10x" only when both sides were measured the same way.
  • Never a trade. Security, tenant isolation, audit and reliability must stay equal or better. A design that weakens them to win on a measure goes to the CTO; it is not a gate answer.

We expect it to work because it uses the review we already run. The Technical Architect checks the section during the critique, and a failing gate is a blocking objection. No new meeting and no new role.

4. Who is affected

ADR authors write four more lines. Internal-only ADRs, such as test tooling, write one line marked N/A. Reviewers get a pass/fail item. The founder gets a running, sourced record of where we are ahead and where we have not proven it yet. Tenants and operators see no change.

5. Risks

  • Most competitor sources are blogs (grade C). A grade-C source on its own may support "we avoid this" but can never get a design sent back.
  • Competitors publish no speed numbers, so "10x faster" stays unproven unless the human allows a benchmark of trial accounts.
  • The section could turn into copy-paste. If two ADRs in a row have identical text, the Solution Architect audits the claims.

6. How we'll know it worked

Mid-way: a docs check in CI fails any new ADR without the section, or one that cites a pain-point ID that does not exist. At the end: the planned measurements exist and produce numbers. These are the timed "first routed record" run, the upgrade-compatibility suite and the cost model.

What the review changed

  • B1. Rev 0 claimed "over 40x faster to first value" by comparing our hours-long setup with competitors' months-long implementation projects. Those are different things, so the claim was removed. The measure is now split into a self-serve measure (competitor side unmeasured) and a go-live measure (our side unmeasured). New rule: no multiplier unless both sides were measured the same way.
  • B2. Rev 0 said the filterable-fields design (ADR-006) avoids Freshworks' "slows down at volume" problem, yet ADR-006 itself accepts slower lists in one band and a timeout error on some filters. It now reads "passes with an accepted risk", naming the CTO acceptance and the revisit trigger.
  • Other improvements:
    • The CI check now runs on every ADR record, not just drafts.
    • "Zero database changes" became "zero tenant-authored database changes", because operators keep one exception.
    • The upgrade promise is scoped to within a major version.
    • Speed comparisons must be timed from the client on both sides.
    • A five-point test now decides when a platform limit is acceptable. It must be needed by an invariant, published, the same for every customer tier, return a clear error before anything happens, and be sized from a measurement.

What is being accepted

  • The speed and first-value "10x" claims stay unproven until competitors are measured the same way.
  • The register rests mostly on blog sources until they are replaced with stronger ones at each six-monthly review.
  • Cost targets wait for the human.

Questions for the human

Q1 (business): Is "one tenth of competitor list price" a goal, and what cost-to-serve target do we set? Do we ever lock features such as the audit log, sandbox or approvals to a pricing tier?
Q2: May we time common actions on free competitor trial accounts? Vendor terms may restrict benchmarking.
